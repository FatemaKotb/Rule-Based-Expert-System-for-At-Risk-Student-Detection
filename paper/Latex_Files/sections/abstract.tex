\begin{center}
    \textbf{ABSTRACT}
\end{center}

\singlespacing

Identifying students at risk of failing or withdrawing early enough to intervene is one of the central problems in learning analytics. Most machine learning approaches to this task produce accurate predictions but offer limited transparency, making it difficult for educators to understand or act on the results. This study builds a rule-based expert system for early at-risk prediction and evaluates it against logistic regression and a support vector machine as baselines. All three systems are trained and tested on module BBB, presentation 2013J from the Open University Learning Analytics Dataset, using only data from the first fourteen weeks of enrolment. The expert system's rules were extracted from a trained decision tree and deployed in an Experta inference engine. The final dataset contained 1,452 students, of whom 33.95\% were at risk. The expert system produced 14 interpretable IF-THEN rules, with average continuous assessment score emerging as the dominant predictor. On the test set, the expert system achieved the highest recall of the three systems (0.778), while the support vector machine achieved the highest accuracy, precision, and F1-score. These results indicate that the rule-based expert system provided competitive predictive performance while preserving full rule-level transparency.
